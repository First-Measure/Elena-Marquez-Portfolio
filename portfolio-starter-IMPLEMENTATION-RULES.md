# Client Portfolio — Implementation Rules

## Design independence — high priority

This portfolio may be produced by First Measure, but it must not look like the First Measure business website or like a recolored First Measure template.

Preserve the approved client palette. Color changes are not the primary method of differentiation.

Treat this starter only as a technical foundation, never as a visual template. Do not carry forward its visual grammar merely because an existing pattern is convenient to reuse.

Avoid reproducing a First Measure-style composition through:

- repeated editorial section layouts;
- repeated bordered cards or panels;
- numbered section systems as a dominant motif;
- identical section-heading treatments throughout the page;
- repeated large editorial statement plus supporting-copy patterns;
- uniform section spacing that gives every section equal importance;
- repeated accent annotations as the primary visual device;
- the same grid/card structure for unrelated content; or
- a sequence of similarly constructed professional-content modules.

## Client-specific visual identity

Derive each portfolio's visual language from the client's profession, audience, personality, and strongest approved evidence. The evidence itself should increasingly become the visual system, using only material that is relevant and supported by the client materials:

- work samples and interface surfaces;
- systems, processes, or architecture relationships;
- decisions, methods, and technical detail;
- representative artifacts where approved;
- outcomes and performance evidence where supported; and
- useful professional metadata.

Integrate this material into the composition rather than placing it inside a generic collection of matching portfolio cards.

Each major area must have a distinct visual job. Establish a clear hierarchy around the client's strongest proof rather than giving every section equal weight. Keep introductory and closing areas restrained relative to the central evidence. Avoid generic SaaS pages, stereotypical profession aesthetics, decorative evidence, logo walls, and visual treatments that do not serve the client's story.

## Required originality check

Before a design revision is considered complete, answer this question:

> If the colors, candidate name, and copy were removed, would the page structure and composition still be readily distinguishable from the First Measure business site?

If the answer is no, the revision has not achieved sufficient design independence. Solve that through composition, hierarchy, evidence presentation, section grammar, grid behavior, typography treatment, and interaction patterns—not by changing colors.

Preserve approved client content, evidence, positioning, and palette while creating a genuinely independent visual identity.
